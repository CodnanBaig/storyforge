# Policy and rights review

Official sources checked **6 November 2026**. Reopen relevant official policies and exact asset-license pages for each real production. This guide defines workflow checks; it does not confer rights or promise a platform decision.

## Originality and repeated formats

YouTube's monetization policy assesses original, authentic content and addresses generic, repetitive or mass-produced output. Review the substance of each episode and the channel's recent output, not just whether the file is newly rendered. Shared visual components or an opening convention should support distinct research, explanation, storytelling and scene action. The workflow must not treat a high posting frequency as proof of quality. [Channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392).

Record what is original about the script, angle, visual interpretation and explanation. Where third-party material is used, assess both permission and the substantive contribution. Passing a copyright check does not by itself establish monetization eligibility.

## Realistic synthetic or altered content

Evaluate the actual visuals, sound and context. YouTube requires disclosure for realistic altered or generated content in the circumstances described by its current policy; clearly non-realistic animation and minor production assistance have different treatment. Preserve a specific disclosure decision and rationale, and revisit it if realistic imagery, voice use or music changes. A reconstruction label does not automatically satisfy every upload setting. [GenAI disclosure guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491).

For a factual film, mark reconstructions as reconstructions where a viewer might mistake them for evidence. Do not label generated images as archival footage. Avoid depicting an identifiable real person committing an unsupported act. Make fiction's framing clear even when its visual style is cinematic.

## Horror, injury and sensitive material

Review the planned visuals, narration, title and thumbnail for the actual niche and audience. Context and intensity matter to advertiser suitability. This library's default for eerie stories is atmospheric, non-graphic treatment, using lighting, sound, scale and uncertainty to build tension. The final review should inspect the actual film; a benign outline does not clear a graphic export. [Advertiser-friendly guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278).

Do not package adult horror as content for small children. Handle real victims, families and unresolved allegations with source discipline and restraint. If a story cannot be told accurately without exploitative presentation, select another angle.

## Rights ledger

Every acquired asset should record its creator/provider, exact source, license or permission, allowed use, attribution requirements, modification history and file hash. For generation services, retain the account plan and terms applicable when the output was created. Do not infer a commercial license from the presence of a voice or an API key.

Music requires the rights applicable to the exact recording and intended use. A composition's public-domain status does not necessarily establish rights to a particular recording. Font files, stock textures, models and reference images also have terms. Record actual licenses instead of relying on filenames or marketing claims.

Remotion has its own software licensing conditions. Eligible individuals and smaller organizations can use its free license under the published terms; other use cases can require a company license. Confirm eligibility for the actual organization and collaboration. [Remotion license and pricing](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/pricing), [license FAQ](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/faq).

## Two review points

**Preflight** happens after research, script, fact checking and direction. It covers facts, originality, planned rights, disclosure, resources, and the authorized budget. It records the intended scope as private preview or commercial. Generation and production wait for this passing state.

**Final QA** examines the actual assets, encoded film, captions, audio and packaging. It rechecks rights and disclosure after any production changes. A passing technical report is required, but it does not replace visual, audio or editorial review. If a material upstream choice changes, invalidate and revalidate its descendants.

The editor produces the exact title, description, credits and any thumbnail before QA. QA records that unchanged approved object as `reviewed_packaging`; the final package must match it. New audience-facing copy after QA requires another review.

The producer resolves routine issues within the user's existing instructions. It asks only when a consequential preference, permission, cost or missing capability cannot be resolved from the brief. Public posting, purchases and account changes require the relevant user authorization; the library's current release stage prepares a package.
